# Phase 3 — Shot Breakdown

Goal: a storyboard where every shot has one job, and the cuts between them feel like one
continuous flow. Analogy: beats are the stations on a train line; shots are the windows
you look out of between stations.

## Step 1 — Story beats (the 6-step spine)

| Beat | Job | Typical shot |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Establish | Space, time, context | Wide / aerial |
| 2. Goal | What do we want? Direction | Medium on the target |
| 3. Enter subject | Bring character to the object | Close-up character / object |
| 4. Reaction | Emotion, the hook | Close-up face |
| 5. Action | Interaction, movement that pushes the story | Medium / handheld |
| 6. Exit | Wide / step into the next scene | Wide, character small in frame |

A 15-shot film is usually this spine played 2-2.5 times with rising stakes.

## Step 2 — Shot key (every shot gets one)

| Field | Question |
|---|---|
| Info | Who, where, what's the target, what's the tension? |
| Action | What does the character physically do, and what changes? |
| Peak | What's the climax or emotional point of this shot? (the frame you'd use as a thumbnail) |

## Step 3 — Camera vocabulary

**Shot size** · EWS extreme wide · WS wide · MS medium (waist up) · MCU medium close (chest up) ·
CU close-up (face) · ECU extreme close-up (eye, hand, object detail) · Insert (object only)

**Lens** · 14-24mm epic/distorted space · 35mm natural, walking with the character ·
50mm neutral human eye · 85mm portrait, emotion, background melts · 100mm macro, textures

**Angle** · eye level · low angle (power) · high angle (vulnerable) · top-down (map/God view) ·
dutch (unease) · over-the-shoulder · POV

**Move** · static/locked · push-in (realization) · pull-out (reveal/isolation) · pan · tilt ·
dolly/track alongside · crane up/down · orbit · handheld (panic) · whip pan (energy cut) ·
FPV drone (speed, flythrough)

## Step 4 — Duration per shot type

| Type | Use | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| Wide | Big space, establishing | 2-4s |
| Medium | Character, group, main action | 1.5-3s |
| Close-up | Emotion, detail, object | 1-2s |
| Action | Fast movement | 0.5-1.5s |
| Extreme close-up | One detail, one feature | 1-2s |

**BPM grid** (cut on the beat so picture and music lock):
- 1 beat = `60 ÷ BPM` s · 1 bar (4 beats) = `240 ÷ BPM` s
- 160 BPM → beat 0.375s, bar 1.5s → shots of 1 bar (1.5s) or 2 bars (3.0s)
- 120 BPM → beat 0.5s, bar 2.0s
- 90 BPM → beat 0.667s, bar 2.67s
Snap every shot to whole beats; climax shots to whole bars. The durations must add up
exactly to the film length.

## Step 5 — Transition rules

| Rule | How |
|---|---|
| Cut on action | Start a motion in shot A, finish it in shot B. Avoid hard cuts on stillness. |
| Change angle | Next shot must change size or angle by a clear step (WS→MCU, not MS→MS). |
| Screen direction | If she runs left→right, she keeps running left→right until a shot shows her turn (180° rule). |
| Pace control | Not too fast all the way. Hold one longer shot before the climax — stillness makes the hit land. |

## Step 6 — Common errors (check every shot)

- Subject is blocked (character or key detail hidden)
- Wrong direction (motion contradicts the previous shot)
- No link between scenes (jump with no bridge — missing an establish or a reaction)
- Unnatural motion (stiff, floaty, physics breaks)
- Too many moving shots in a row (everything moves = nothing reads)

## Clean-shot checklist

☐ clear angle ☐ clear action line ☐ spaces connect ☐ pace is reasonable ☐ one peak moment
per shot
